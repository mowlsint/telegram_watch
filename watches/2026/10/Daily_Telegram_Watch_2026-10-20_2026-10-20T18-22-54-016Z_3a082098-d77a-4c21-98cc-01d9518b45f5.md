Die Schwerpunkte liegen auf einem Tankerzwischenfall in der westlichen Ostsee, neuen Schadensinformationen zum Petersburger Ölterminal, zwei schweren Bootsunglücken in der Ägäis sowie Entwicklungen der europäischen maritimen Sicherheits- und Verteidigungsinfrastruktur.

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY – ENGLISH

Several new maritime developments were identified during the reporting period. The principal subjects are a tanker incident in the western Baltic Sea, newly published damage information concerning the St Petersburg Oil Terminal, two fatal maritime accidents in the Aegean Sea and developments affecting European maritime security and defence infrastructure.

The Bahamas-flagged crude oil tanker SLED (IMO 9284805) resumed its voyage through the Kadet Trench on 19 October. The vessel had suffered propulsion problems between Rügen and the Danish island of Møn the previous day. It was carrying Russian crude oil from Primorsk towards Skagen. German and Danish authorities monitored the incident, and German emergency tug VB BREMEN FIGHTER was deployed. Russian naval vessels, including landing ship ALEKSANDR SHABALIN, were also present in the wider area. An escort relationship with the tanker has not been established. No oil pollution or injuries were reported.

A separate report concerned an unusual flag change by tanker MYRA (IMO 9336490) in the western Baltic Sea. The vessel reportedly changed its AIS-transmitted flag from Madagascar to Cameroon during its voyage. German Federal Police monitored its movements. The underlying event occurred before the current reporting period. The vessel's actual registration status remains unverified.

New satellite-image assessments of the 18 October drone attack against the St Petersburg Oil Terminal were published on 19 October. According to Militarnyi and Telegram channel Absolutely Reliable Source, three storage tanks were damaged, including one reportedly destroyed. These findings originate from a published imagery assessment and have not been independently confirmed. The consequences for maritime cargo operations remain unknown.

Twelve people died in two separate maritime accidents near Lesbos and Samos in the Aegean Sea. On the evening of 18 October, a speedboat carrying migrants collided with a Greek Coast Guard vessel near Lesbos. Six people died, including an infant. Another speedboat capsized north of Samos during the morning of 19 October, killing six people. Greek authorities conducted search and rescue operations.

Rheinmetall and Swiss shipping company MSC confirmed on 19 October that they had won the auction to acquire Romania's Mangalia shipyard for approximately €138 million. The facility is intended to become a dual-use civilian and military shipbuilding centre. Planned projects include two offshore patrol vessels and two diver-support vessels for Romania.

Denmark ordered additional Naval Strike Missiles for its land-based coastal defence system. The contract, announced on 19 October, is worth approximately NOK 400 million. It supplements the coastal defence acquisition agreed in 2025.

EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas also outlined the planned revision of Europe's Arctic strategy on 19 October. Proposed measures include stronger surveillance of suspicious shipping, submarine cable protection and a maritime security hub. The strategy had not yet been formally adopted.

No new confirmed attacks against merchant vessels in the Black Sea, damage to European submarine cables or pipelines, or vessel-specific GNSS interference incidents were established.
